# Special Training College
The Special Training College (STC) scholarship for a vocational course in Japan lasts for 3 years. After 1 year of preparatory courses and Japanese language learning, the student will enter a vocational school for a two-year course. This scholarship aims to provide the necessary skills and knowledge for professional life. All classes are taught in Japanese.

<Warning>
The information in this article refers to JASSO students. STC students who go to Tokyo usually take their courses with Bunka, and differences may arise between the two. If you are a STC/Bunka student and would like to contribute, please send a message on our [Discord server]({siteConfig.discord_invite}).
</Warning>

# Preparatory Year
The first year of the scholarship consists of a preparatory course. 

The first thing you will do upon arriving in Japan is take a placement test. Classes are split into five levels based on Japanese fluency.

All classes use the same material, but the advanced classes start further ahead, and the learning targets are more advanced too.
That also means that if you end up in a class where the average student is at a higher level than you, you will have to find a way to keep up.

Moreover, **the tests don't have a fixed date**; it depends on the homeroom teacher.

## Japanese Classes
The Japanese classes are fairly standardized.

The first period is always dictation (書き取り).
The teacher reads out sentences from the texts you are studying (sometimes making small changes if they're in the mood, just to force you to write something specific). Then you have to write the sentence down, using all the kanji (漢字) from that sentence, and you have to write the furigana (振り仮名) for everything.

The first period also includes recitation (暗唱), which is basically where you memorize a short Japanese conversation and perform it with a random person in the class. A kanji test may also be held.

Other than that, classes cover whatever your advisor or the instructor in charge deems necessary, such as studying extra N2 grammar points, learning kanji beyond the ones in the book; writing essays, and giving presentations.

The other periods follow the textbook. It follows the same structure overall:
- Read a paragraph/essay;
- Learn the new grammar explanation;
- Make sentences using the new grammar;
- Learn the kanji;
- Repeat.

<Note>
In beginner classes, an exam is held every **10 chapters**. Once you reach the intermediate level, exams are held every **5 chapters**.
</Note>

<Note>
Even the most advanced class does not start at such an advanced level.
The major difference is that if you're in a class where everyone already has N2/N1, they will breeze through those initial chapters much faster.
</Note>

## Additional Classes
Around June, there's a test to determine placement for classes in other subjects (listed below). After summer break, classes for those other subjects start too.

<Note>
The Mathematics class for the Natural Sciences and the Humanities tracks are in different levels.
</Note>
    
### Natural Sciences
For the Natural Sciences (Mathematics/STEM) track, those additional subjects include English, Mathematics, Japanese Culture, Japanese Caligraphy (書道) and Physics.

### Social Sciences
For the Natural Sciences (Mathematics/STEM) track, those additional subjects include English, Mathematics, Japanese Culture, Japanese Caligraphy (書道) and Sociology.
